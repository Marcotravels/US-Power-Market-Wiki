## Part III: Recommended New Research Topics

### Tier 1: High Priority for Environmental Economics PhD

#### 1. Carbon Pricing Integration in US Wholesale Electricity Markets

**Rationale:** This is the most significant gap. A PhD researcher in environmental economics would logically investigate how carbon pricing interacts with electricity market design.

**Research Questions:**
- How do existing state carbon pricing programs (CA cap-and-trade, RGGI) affect wholesale electricity prices and dispatch decisions?
- What is the pass-through rate of carbon costs to LMP in organized markets?
- How should FERC incorporate carbon pricing into market rules given federalism constraints?
- What are the efficiency implications of fragmented state carbon pricing vs federal carbon pricing?

**Relevant Frameworks:**
- Internalization of externalities in wholesale markets
- Second-best pricing in network industries
- Incidence of carbon taxation

**Data Sources:** CAISO, PJM, MISO market clearing data; RGGI auction data; EPA eGRID

---

#### 2. Capacity Market Design and Resource Adequacy in Energy Transition

**Rationale:** As renewable penetration increases, capacity markets face fundamental questions about how to value intermittent resources and ensure resource adequacy.

**Research Questions:**
- How do capacity market outcomes in PJM compare economically to energy-only outcomes in ERCOT?
- What is the optimal capacity mechanism design for high-renewable systems?
- How should capacity credits be assigned to solar, wind, and storage?
- What are the welfare implications of capacity market price caps?

**Relevant Frameworks:**
- Reliability option pricing theory
- Option value in capacity markets
- Missing money problem

**Data Sources:** PJM capacity market filings, RPM auction results; ERCOT long-term assessments

---

#### 3. PJM vs ERCOT: A Comparative Economic Analysis of Market Design Outcomes

**Rationale:** The wiki documents treat PJM and ERCOT separately but don't compare their economic outcomes. A comparative analysis is central to understanding market design effects on consumer welfare and investment.

**Research Questions:**
- How do wholesale electricity prices compare between PJM (LMP + capacity market) and ERCOT (energy-only, system marginal price)?
- Which market structure better protects consumers during extreme weather events?
- What are the investment incentive differences for generation and storage?
- How do price volatility patterns differ, and what are the welfare implications?

**Relevant Frameworks:**
- Comparative institutional analysis
- Market design theory (Mechanism design)
- Event study methodology for extreme weather impacts

**Data Sources:** PJM and ERCOT market data portals; EIA 923 data

---

#### 4. Cross-Border Electricity Trade and the Economics of Interconnection

**Rationale:** The wiki notes that ERCOT is "not connected" to Eastern/Western interconnections, citing the 2021 winter storm as evidence of vulnerability. But there's no economic analysis of what trade is possible or how it would improve welfare.

**Research Questions:**
- What is the economic value of additional interconnection capacity between ERCOT and Eastern Interconnection?
- How do current HVDC ties affect price convergence across interconnections?
- What are the welfare effects of ERCOT's isolation policy?
- How would expanded trade affect renewable integration and price stabilization?

**Relevant Frameworks:**
- International trade theory applied to electricity
- Arbitrage and price convergence
- Option value of interconnection

**Data Sources:** EIA trade data, FERC Form 714, ERCOT interchange data
